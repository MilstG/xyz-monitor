What you already decided about this name

The board tells you what a name is doing. It has never held what you concluded about it — the level you're waiting for, the reason you sized down, the thesis you bought in March. That lives in a text file or nowhere. This adds notes per ticker, written in the drawer, and a post-it marker in the markets table so you can see which names you've already thought about without opening any of them. The one idea that makes it more than a scratchpad: a note is stamped with the price you wrote it at, so the app can tell you the move since — and fade the marker as the note ages out of relevance.

The marker sits in the sticky ticker cell beside ★ and the E badge, for the reason styles.css already writes down for the E badge: that cell is the only one always on screen, and a marker you have to scroll sideways to reach is not a marker. The glyph is a shape, not a dot — the folded corner is what makes it read as a written note rather than another status light at 11px, next to a rail, a star, a badge and a countdown that are all already competing in that cell. Absence is drawn as absence: a name with no note shows nothing at all, because a permanent hollow outline on every row would sit next to the ☆ and mean nothing.

Still yours, but the name has had a month to disagree with you. Read it before you act on it.

Old enough that it is history, not guidance. It is never deleted and never hidden — it just stops shouting.
more than one — counted
BTC3
The count appears only past one, and the colour always tracks the newest note — three stale notes must not read as a live one.

Why fade at all. The failure mode of every notes feature is the note you wrote at $94 still reading as advice at $118. Age is the cheapest honest signal available, and it costs no extra data: the marker is bright while the note is probably still true, dim while it might be, and a hollow outline once it is a record rather than a plan. Nothing is ever auto-deleted — the app dims your notes, it does not throw them away. The clock is calendar time, and only calendar time. The alternative — scaling the fade by the name's own volatility, so a note on a 90%-vol perp ages faster than one on a 15%-vol utility — is rejected: see below. The 7d / 30d thresholds themselves are a starting proposal, not a finding.

…the rest of the drawer is unchanged: sparkline, candles, series, ledger, news, metrics.

Notes sit above the charts, not at the bottom. Everything else in the drawer is the server's read of the name. The note is yours, and it is the thing you want to hit before you re-derive an opinion from the same metrics that produced it last time. Placing it under the sparkline would make it a footnote to the data; placing it above makes the data the second opinion.

Empty costs one line. With no notes the panel is a single ＋ note button and one line of muted text — it never occupies drawer height it hasn't earned, which is why it can afford to sit this high.

The stamp is the price, not just the time. Saving freezes the current mark into the note. That single field is what lets every later read say +18.4% since instead of just a date, turns the Notes tab into a record you can be wrong in front of, and costs one number per note.

Editing keeps the original stamp. An edited note shows · edited and still measures from the price you first wrote it at — the claim was made then. Rewriting the stamp would let you quietly move your own goalposts, which is the one thing a self-scoring record must not allow.

4 · The Notes tab  every note across every name, newest first

Search the bodies · click a #tag to filter · click any row to open that ticker's drawer.

A note outlives its market. The last row is on a name that is no longer in the universe — a market that got delisted, renamed, or rotated out of the xyz dex. It is kept, greyed, and labelled, because the note is a record of what you thought, and a record that silently drops rows is not one. Notes are keyed by coin and displayed by ticker, so a rename moves the note with the market instead of orphaning it.

5 · What this pins down

Notes are content, not preferences

The watchlist, layouts and column prefs live in localStorage, which is right: they are a view, and losing them costs you a re-click. A note is something a person sat and typed. Clearing a browser or opening the board on a phone must not lose it.

So notes are CONFIG-grade and server-side, exactly like baskets.json: written to $DATA_DIR/notes.json with the same atomic tmp-then-rename, warm-loaded on boot, surviving redeploys — and group-shared behind the same isAdmin write gate baskets already use.

The price stamp is the feature

Without it this is a text box, and a text box is what the app is trying to replace. With it, every note carries the move since it was written, the markers can decay honestly, and the Notes tab becomes a plain-language ledger that sits next to the machine one.

It is also nearly free: the snapshot already holds the mark, so writing a note costs one extra number and reading it costs one subtraction.

Age is shown, never enforced

The marker fades and the note grows a "written 94d ago" line. Nothing expires, nothing is archived, nothing prompts you to "review" anything. A stale note is still evidence about how you were thinking, which is the part worth keeping.

Notes age in days, not in volatility

The tempting version scales the fade by the name's own vol, so a note on a 90%-vol perp greys out faster than one on a sleepy utility. It is rejected, for one reason: the marker would then change when the market changed, not when anything about your note changed. A note you wrote on Tuesday would brighten through Thursday because realised vol came in. A marker that moves on its own is not a marker.

Calendar time is also the unit you already think in. You remember writing something last week; nobody has ever recalled a thought in vol-adjusted units. And it needs no input beyond the timestamp — nothing to compute, nothing to be stale, nothing to explain in a tooltip.

One marker, three states, no new column

The ticker cell already carries a session rail, a star, an E badge and a countdown microline. The post-it earns its place there by encoding three things in one 11px glyph — exists, how fresh, how many — and by being absent the rest of the time.

6 · What is missing before this ships

The route does not exist

GET /api/notes and POST /api/notes ({coin, body} creates, {id, body} edits, {id, drop:true} deletes) — one route, verbs in the body, matching the shape /api/baskets already uses so the admin gate covers all of them at once. Storage is a solved problem in this repo; none of it is written for notes yet.

The table needs a digest, not the bodies

Painting the markers must not cost a fetch per row. The snapshot should carry a tiny per-coin digest — nt:{n, ts, px}, three fields — enough for the glyph, its age class and the hover line. Full bodies stay behind /api/notes and load with the drawer.

Still open

Where exactly the 7d / 30d thresholds sit. The axis is settled — see Notes age in days — but the two numbers are a starting proposal and want a few weeks of real notes before anyone defends them.

Should a note be attachable to a ledger claim, so "why I took this" and the scored outcome sit together? That is the natural next step and deliberately out of scope here.
