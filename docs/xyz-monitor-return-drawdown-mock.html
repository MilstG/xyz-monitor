Return since a date, against the pain it cost

The table says where a name is now. The drawer sparkline shows the path. Neither answers the question you actually ask when you pick a date and look back: of everything that went up since then, which ones went up without first making you sick? One dot per market — the return since the anchor, up, against the deepest drawdown in the same window, right, with zero drawdown at the right edge. Up and to the right is better: more return for less pain. The benchmarks are dashed lines at their own return, so "beat the market" and "beat it without the hole" read at once.

2 · The references, and the cut  what turns a scatter into a verdict, and what keeps it readable

The benchmarks are horizontal lines

BTC and ETH in crypto scope, the S&P and the XYZ100 index in stocks scope: each is a dashed line at its own return, named at the left edge, and its dot wears the colour. Everything above a line beat that reference; everything above it and to its right beat it with a shallower hole — the two questions a relative-return chart answers one at a time, read together.

Best / DD in the table is the other read: above 1 the name made more than it ever gave back inside the window. It was a diagonal on the chart in an earlier draft; on a full board the line crossed everything and said nothing the column does not, so it went.

Top N is a cut on the chart, never on the study

Show top draws the top 10, 20, 30 or 50 names by the return axis — the references always stay, however badly they rank — because a hundred labelled dots is a texture, not a chart. The table underneath is never cut: the whole universe, 25 rows a page, sortable on every column, so nothing the cut hides is out of reach.

Why the drawdown is measured from the running peak

A name that went +40% and then fell to +10% has a −21% drawdown, not −30 points of "given back" — the two are different questions. Gave back (best minus now) is in the table because it is what a holder feels; the drawdown on the axis is the path-dependent one, because the path is what a stop would have hit.

Both are stated in the readout with their dates, so a dot is never a number without a story.

4 · What this pins down

The anchor is a UTC midnight, and the first bar on or after it is the base

Daily bars are keyed by UTC day, so "since March 3" resolves to the first close at or after 2026-03-03T00:00Z. A weekend anchor starts on the Monday and is not flagged; a first bar more than four days past the anchor is a listing that came after it and wears a dashed ring, marked late, and starts at its own first close — it is in the picture but it is not the same race.

The live mark is today's point

Between daily-bar refreshes a name making its high right now would otherwise read as yesterday's close. The mark is folded in as the last point (replacing today's forming bar when one exists), so best, drawdown and now all move with the tape. The readout says live mark or last close so the source is never ambiguous.

Closes only, and the caption says so

The daily feed carries no intraday low, so the drawdown is close-to-close: a wick below the close is not counted. A study that quietly used highs for the numerator and closes for the denominator would flatter every name; this one uses closes for both and states it under the chart. The return axis is now by default and best one click away: the first is where the name sits, the second is the amplitude of the excursion, and the table carries both.

Labels route, dots never move

A dot's position is the data. Labels take the sector map's greedy layout — a free side, a leader line when it is not the default one, a page-colour halo — so a crowded board stays legible without a single dot being nudged. Past 45 names auto keeps the standouts on either axis, the best ratios and the references; all and none override, and the references stay named under none.

No route, no persistence, no server

The study is a pure function of /api/daily, already pinned public under the markets key, plus the live mark. Gating the tab can never strand a request; the anchor, label mode and sort live in this browser's localStorage and nowhere else.

Follows the Stocks | Crypto switcher

Same study, the other universe's closes. Crypto history on the wire is about 90 days, so an older anchor starts every coin late — the header counts them rather than drawing a full board that silently means something else.

5 · What it deliberately does not do

No benchmark-relative axis

"Return vs the S&P" is a different chart (the Sectors leaders map already has one). The benchmark is a line on this one, not a subtraction: absolute return stays on the axis and the reference stays visible as itself.

No bubble size

Volume as a third channel made the crowded board unreadable and answered nothing the question asked. Every dot is 10px; the hit target is 22px.

No intraday anchor

The finest anchor is a UTC day. An intraday version needs the hourly spine, which is per-name and lazily fetched; the daily one is on every page already.
