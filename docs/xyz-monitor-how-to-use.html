How to use this site

Feature by feature: what it is worth to you, how to use it properly, and the small habits that make it pay. Read it top to bottom once, then come back to whichever section you are standing in. If you want the ideas behind the app first, read the explainer; if you want the exact definition of a number, the manual has it.

Nothing here is investment advice. The site measures and shows; what you do with it is yours.

00A daily routine, if you want one

You do not have to use everything. Most people settle into one of these three loops; each takes a few minutes and touches four or five features. Pick one, and let the rest of this page fill in the detail.

The five-minute open

  1. Markets, window 1d, sort by vs tape.
  2. Glance at the 1d movers chips and the action strip's HEATING list.
  3. Calendar: who reports today, what macro prints today.
  4. Bell: anything fired overnight?
  5. Open one name, write one line in Notes.

The positioning read

  1. Funding heatmap, 8h: who is paying, and is it building or fading?
  2. Markets, sort by Squeeze and by OI/Vol.
  3. Sectors flow map: which theme is being bought with conviction.
  4. Correlation: is the trade you like already the trade you have?
  5. Set a rule on the level you care about.

The weekly review

  1. Sessions: is the overnight edge still there net of funding?
  2. Signals: search your favourite names' claim histories.
  3. Messages › calls ↗: how are your calls scoring?
  4. Notes: re-read what you thought, with the move since.
  5. Trim the watchlist. Save the layout.

01The Markets table

Value

One screen that answers "what is the tape doing, and which names are doing something different" — with every number pre-computed so the page is instant and everyone sees the same thing.

How to use it
  1. Pick your scope first. Stocks | Crypto at the top. Nothing carries across the wall, so if a column looks empty, check which side you are on.
  2. Set the window (1h · 4h · 1d · 7d · 30d). Every column that says "over the window" moves together: vs S&P, vs tape, ΔOI, Squeeze, Carry, Avg Range, RVOL. Use 1d for the day, 7d for the week's story, 30d for positioning that has had time to build.
  3. Sort by clicking a header. Click again to flip. The most useful sorts are not price changes: try vs tape (who is strong on a weak day), RVOL (who is being traded unusually), ΔOI (where money is arriving) and Squeeze.
  4. Filter the noise. Filters holds volume and open-interest floors (in millions; 500k and 2.5b work too). A floor of a few million in 24h volume removes the names you could not exit at size.
  5. Show the columns you actually use. Columns shows, hides and drag-reorders. Several useful ones are hidden by default: the moving averages, VWAP, the intraday opens, DownCap.
  6. Hover before you conclude. Every cell tells you its inputs, its window and its sample size. A shaded cell scales with the size of the move, so a wall of deep red 7d cells is the breadth read.
  7. Export with ↓ CSV — you get exactly the rows, columns, sort and filters on screen.
The two lenses most people miss
  • group → sectors / industries. The same table with one row per sector. Each cell is the weighted average of the rows you are showing — and Best · Worst tells you whether the sector number is everyone or one name dragging the rest. Click a group row to drill into its members.
  • The action strip under the table (click ▸ Action). HEATING is what is starting: acceleration confirmed by flow. COOLING is what is still ahead of the tape but has stopped moving — with a high momentum score that is the take-profit read. STRONGEST BID is who got bought on the dip in the last four hours.
Worked exampleWindow 7d, sort vs tape descending, volume floor 5. The top of the table is what held up when the tape did not. Now hover the ΔOI cell on the top three: longs+ means new money confirmed it, squeeze means it was shorts covering — a different trade entirely.
In real life — a red Tuesday

It is 9:45 ET and the tape is down 1.5%. Sorting by 24h just shows you who fell least. Instead you set the window to 1d and sort vs tape descending.

Three names are green against a red tape. The top one, HOOD, also shows RVOL 2.4× and ΔOI +9% longs+: it is being bought, with size, while everything else is sold.

So: that is the one name you open. The action strip's HEATING list agrees. You have found the day's relative-strength candidate in about forty seconds, without a single hover.

02The ticker drawer

Value

Everything the site knows about one name, on one scroll, so you stop tab-hopping to build a picture.

How to use it
  1. Open it by clicking a row, pressing Enter on a highlighted row, or Ctrl/⌘+K and typing the ticker.
  2. Read it top to bottom. Header and event risk first (is there an earnings print or a macro event inside your horizon?), then where the 30d return happened (off-hours vs cash session), then the metrics grid, then candles and the OI / funding history, then news and the signal record.
  3. Use the focus chip. Opening a name sets the ◎ focused ticker. Now Trend, Charts, Report and Correlation open on that same name — click the chip in the status line to reopen the drawer from anywhere.
  4. Write while you are there. The Notes panel sits above the charts. That is on purpose: everything else in the drawer is the server's read; the note is yours.
  • The 30d split is the underrated panel. A name that made all its return overnight is a different hold from one that made it during the session — and the funding you pay to hold overnight is right beside it.
  • Co-movers and hedges are computed on 90 days of daily returns. Before you add a name, check whether it is a co-mover of something you already hold.
  • The signal record is the out-of-sample history of every claim the engine made on this name. A name with a poor record is a name where the engine's flags deserve less weight.
  • The URL follows you. #t=NVDA in the address bar is a shareable link straight to this drawer.
In real life — sizing before you click buy

You open HOOD from the table. Event risk says it reports Thursday after the close; the reaction study shows a median move of 7.1% the next session, 2.3× its usual range. The 30d split says most of this month's return came off-hours.

So: any position is sized for a 7% gap, not a 3% day. You decide either to close before Thursday, or to hold half size through the print on purpose. Either way the decision was made before the trade, with numbers, in one scroll.

03Watchlist and saved layouts

Value

The table shaped the way you read it, on every device, without redoing it each morning.

How to use it
  1. Star names with the ★ beside the ticker. Starred names float to the top of the table; ★ only in Filters shows nothing else. The watchlist follows your account, so your phone has it too.
  2. Arrange the table once — columns, order, sort, window, volume/OI floors, ★-only.
  3. Layouts → save, and give it a name that says what it is for: positioning, open, swing.
  4. Switch layouts from the same menu. A • beside the active name means you have drifted from what you saved; save again or switch back.
  • Make a layout per question, not per day. A "positioning" layout with Funding, ΔOI, Squeeze, OI/Vol and Carry; a "strength" layout with vs tape, RVOL, DownCap and Momentum. Switching layouts is faster than re-sorting.
  • Saved layouts sync; the active one does not. Your phone can run its own layout while the desktop runs another — that is deliberate, not a bug.
  • New columns are never dropped from an old layout. When the app adds a column, your saved view gains it (hidden if it is hidden by default) rather than silently losing it.
  • The ticker search box is not part of a layout — it is meant to be typed and cleared.
In real life — twelve names, two questions

You actively follow twelve names. You star them. Then you save two layouts: positioning (Funding, ΔOI, Squeeze, OI/Vol, Carry) and strength (vs tape, RVOL, DownCap, Momentum).

Monday on the phone: ★ only plus the positioning layout is your whole book's crowding on one screen. At the desk, the strength layout is where the ideas come from.

So: no more rebuilding the table every morning, and the phone stays on its own compact layout while the desktop keeps the wide one.

04Trend

Value

Separates "it went up" from "it is trending" — and points at the pullback, which is where an entry with a defined risk actually lives.

How to use it
  1. Pick a side — the long board or the short board.
  2. Read the score. Each name gets a rung per timeframe (daily, 12h, 4h, 1h); green means price is above a stacked EMA 13/21 ribbon on that rung. 4/4 is an established trend on every timeframe; 2–3/4 usually means the higher timeframes lead and the lower ones are pulling back.
  3. Look for RETEST. The badge means price just probed into the ribbon while the close held the right side — the continuation pullback. The volume multiple beside it tells you whether the level is being fought (≥2×) or quietly respected (~1×).
  4. Check Width and Age. Two 4/4 trends are not the same trade when one rides a hair-thin ribbon and the other a wide one. Age is how many days the daily rung has been stacked — a day-3 trend is an entry, a day-40 trend is a chase. The board ranks fresh-first for exactly that reason.
  5. Click the chart button on a row to see the rung with the ribbon and the retest zone drawn at the ladder's own levels.
  • Δ21 is the live distance from the hourly EMA21 — your proximity-to-entry number.
  • The badge you see is the same condition that becomes a signal in the ledger. The board and the signal cannot disagree.
  • A name missing a rung (too little history) is excluded, not guessed — the header counts them.
In real life — the pullback, not the chase

NVDA sits on the long board at 3/4, RETEST · 1.1× vol, age 4d, Δ21 −0.3%. The daily, 12h and 4h are stacked; the hourly just dipped into the ribbon and held.

Four days old means it is an entry, not a chase. 1.1× volume means nobody is fighting the level. Δ21 says price is a third of a percent from the hourly EMA21 — the level you would risk against.

So: you buy near the hourly EMA21 with the void just under the ribbon. A defined-risk pullback in an established trend, found in one glance, rather than a day-40 trend you would be chasing.

05Charts and Treemap

Value

Charts: compare shapes across names or across timeframes without opening eight drawers. Treemap: the whole universe in one glance, sized by what matters to you.

Charts, how to use it
  1. GRID puts up to eight names side by side. Choose panes (4, 6 or 8), then type into add ticker… to fill them; each pane keeps its own timeframe.
  2. MTF WALL puts one name across timeframes — the same day seen at 15m, 1h, 4h and 1d (more rungs with more panes).
  3. LINK shares the crosshair and zoom across panes (always on in the wall), so hovering one bar lines up the same moment everywhere. RESET returns every pane to its default window.
  4. EMA overlays two moving averages of your choosing on every pane; SAVE keeps the whole arrangement as a named chart layout for this browser.
  • Short timeframes read the local 5-minute archive; 4h and above read the deep daily archive. Each pane says which — they are never blended in one chart.
  • Warm-up bars for an EMA stay empty rather than plotting an unconverged line.
Treemap, how to use it
  1. Choose size — 24h volume or open interest. Volume shows where the activity is; OI shows where the positioning is.
  2. Choose the window — colour is the move over it.
  3. Click a tile to open the name.
  • Size by OI and window 7d is the fastest way to see where a week of red actually hurt.
In real life — is it the stock or the sector?

MU drops 3% in an hour. Before deciding it is a Micron story you open Charts, GRID, four panes: MU, NVDA, AMD, AVGO, 1h, LINK on.

Hovering the red bar on MU lines up the same hour on all four: they all dropped together. That is a semis move, not a Micron move — and the treemap sized by OI, window 1d, shows the whole tile block red.

So: you do not go looking for Micron news that is not there; you look at what hit the sector.

06Sectors

Value

Tells you which theme is being bought or sold — and whether the theme trades as one block or as a bucket of stock picks.

How to use it
  1. Start on the flow map. Each bubble is a sector. Right = money flowing in with conviction (price plus open interest); up = activity (volume plus volatility); size = 24h volume. Top-right is accumulation, top-left is distribution, the bottom half is quiet.
  2. Flip to Leaders to see who is beating the benchmark (right) and whether the lead is growing (up) or fading. "Catching up" is where early rotation shows; "Cooling" is where leadership goes to die.
  3. Read Cohesion on the rotation board. High = the label trades as one thing, so any member will do; low = a stock-picker's sector where the name matters more than the theme.
  4. Click a bubble for its members with their own contribution to the composite.
  5. Try equal-weight. By volume, a mega-cap can be the whole sector's story; equal-weight shows whether the rest agree.
  • The sector × sector correlation matrix catches one risk factor wearing two names — Semis and Software moving together is one bet, not two.
  • Grouping switches from GICS sectors to curated industries (Semis vs Software vs Memory). Industries are display-only; the signal engine keeps pooling on the eleven sectors.
In real life — buying the theme vs picking the name

The map shows Semis in the accumulation corner and Software in distribution. On the rotation board, Semis has Cohesion 0.78; Software has 0.31.

High cohesion means the semis are moving as one block — any liquid semi gets you the theme. Low cohesion in software means the label is meaningless; some names are being bought and some dumped under the same word.

So: for semis you pick by liquidity and take the theme. For software you click the bubble, look at the members' contributions, and pick the one actually in the flow. Same tab, two different ways to act.

07Correlation

Value

Stops you from owning the same trade three times under three tickers — and shows you the natural hedge when you want one.

How to use it
  1. Matrix. Names are cluster-ordered, so the blocks along the diagonal are real co-movement families. Hover a cell for the correlation and the number of days behind it — a ±0.9 on twelve days is noise. Use the focus search to isolate a few names; widen or narrow with top by vol.
  2. Click a cell for the pair view: the hedge ratio (β, units of B per unit of A), the spread between them with its mean and ±1σ band, and the z-score. Beyond ±1.5–2 one side is rich or cheap against the other. The rolling correlation underneath says whether the relationship is stable enough to trust.
  3. Click a ticker label for its co-movers (proxies and contagion) on one side and its natural hedges on the other.
  4. COMP/G overlays several names rebased to 100 at an anchor you choose — presets, a date, or drag the amber line. Spread mode shows each name minus the basket of the visible names.
  5. Ratio draws synthetic pair candles (A divided by B) with a properly honest EMA200 — drawn only where 200 bars exist. BTC may face a stock here, aligned on the hours both traded.
Worked exampleYou like a long in a chip name and already hold another one. Click the matrix cell for the pair: a 0.85 correlation on 90 days with the spread at +1.8σ says the one you want is already rich against the one you own. That is a pair trade, not a second long.
  • Type comp AAPL MSFT NVDA or ratio NVDA/BTC 4h into the Ask console to jump straight to these views.
In real life — the second chip stock

You are long NVDA and like AMD too. In the matrix, the NVDA–AMD cell reads ρ 0.85 on 90 days. Click it: the spread sits at +1.8σ — AMD is rich against NVDA.

Adding AMD would be the same bet twice, and at a bad relative price. The hedges panel for NVDA shows the name that has moved against it most consistently.

So: either you skip AMD, or you turn it into a pair (short the rich one, long the cheap one) — or you keep NVDA and cut some beta with the hedge. What you do not do is buy AMD thinking it is a new idea.

08Funding heatmap

Value

Carry is a cost or an income on every perp you hold. This screen shows who is paying, across the whole book, over days — so you can see crowding building or fading rather than a single number.

How to use it
  1. Read the colours. Red = longs pay (the crowd is long; holding costs money). Green = longs receive. Every row is also labelled with its signed number, so you never depend on colour alone.
  2. Pick a resolution — 1h, 8h or 24h. In the default annualized unit, the timeframe is only a zoom: one market reads one number on every grid.
  3. Compare now against the mean. Each row ends with its window mean and its current funding in the same unit. Current above mean = carry building; below = fading.
  4. Sort by carry side, by |carry|, on the mean or on the now column.
  5. Switch to per-bucket only when you want the actual amount a 1× long paid over a bucket — that unit changes with the timeframe (about 8× larger per 8h than per 1h).
  • Hatched cells are honest gaps — the data covered under half the bucket's hours. They are never drawn as "flat", because a gap and a genuinely flat market mean opposite things to anyone sizing a position.
  • The newest column is always the last complete bucket.
  • Rows rank by open interest; show top 25, 50 or everything.
In real life — what the overnight actually costs

You are long HOOD and it is Thursday. Its row on the 8h grid has been red all week; the window mean is +58% and the now column is +71%. The crowd is paying more every day to be long.

71% annualized is roughly 0.2% of notional per day. On a name you expect to move 2–3% a week, that is a real slice of the expected return going out the door.

So: you either accept the cost knowingly, size down, or wait for the now column to fall back under the mean before adding. The point is you saw the bill before it arrived.

09Sessions

Value

Answers when the returns actually happen — overnight, over the weekend, or during the cash session — net of the funding it costs to be there.

How to use it
  1. Start with the decomposition. Three equity curves: what an overnight hold (close→open), a weekend hold and a cash-session hold each paid, pooled across the equity universe.
  2. Watch the shaded band. That is the running funding drag. An edge that dies inside the band is a donation.
  3. Read the clocks — hour-of-day activity in each market's own norm, the funding clock, the day-of-week heatmap — with sample-size humility; hover for the breadth behind every point.
  • A rising overnight curve against a flat cash curve is the classic overnight effect. The drawer's where the 30d return happened panel is the same question asked of one name.
  • Panels that need more history than exists yet stay empty rather than showing thin data.
  • None of this exists in crypto scope — a 24/7 tape has no sessions.
In real life — the cautious habit that was costing money

You have been flattening at 4pm ET on principle — no overnight risk. The decomposition says overnight holds returned about +6% over the period, net of the funding band, while the cash session was flat.

So: your caution has been selling the part of the day that paid and keeping the part that did not. You do not have to change everything — but the drawer's 30d split on your own names is now the first thing you check before deciding when to hold.

10Signals

Value

A ranked list of unusual conditions with their measured history attached — and a public record of how every past flag turned out, so you can decide how much to trust the next one.

How to read a card
  1. Line 1 — the event, a plain-language reading, when it appeared, and the claim it is scored against (the frozen mark and the time to resolution).
  2. Line 2 — the evidence, always in the same order: scope · n · median outcome · hit rate · expectancy · horizon. n is the number that matters. A pill saying unproven means no history; neg exp means past occurrences lost money on average.
  3. Line 3 — the mechanical play: side, target, the level that voids it, reward:risk. These are frozen when the claim opens and never move.
  4. Line 4 — the one corroborating thing to watch.
How to use it
  1. Sort by score, but read n first. Score is unusualness now plus historical edge; a high score on n=3 is a story, not a base rate.
  2. Prefer ★ prime — ≥60% hit, positive expectancy and sound structure at fire time.
  3. Mind the decay. A signal past its claimed horizon dims (amber) and drops at twice the horizon. A stale signal is not a signal.
  4. Check the earnings guard. A card flagged for a print inside the horizon has its evidence capped, on purpose, and says so.
  5. Search a ticker to see its complete claim-by-claim history — what fired, at what mark, what it claimed, what happened. Do this before you trust any card on that name.
  6. Look at the accuracy panel occasionally: live hit vs claimed, the profit factor (which catches the 55%-hit signal that still loses money), and the calibration buckets that audit the ranking itself.
  • ⇄ on a card means long and short fired on the same name — flagged, and nobody gets a confluence bonus.
  • Confluence (several same-side conditions on one name) only earns a bonus once the record proves agreement helps. It is measured, not assumed.
  • The Actionable taboperator-only by default is the subset of these claims that are at their trigger right now, with reward:risk net of funding.
In real life — a card worth acting on, and one that is not

Card A: FUNDING EXTREME · HOOD · short, n 23, hit 65%, +0.9R, ★ prime, void 117.2. You search HOOD's history: 18 past claims, 61% hit. That is a base rate.

Card B, higher score: BREAKOUT · a thin FX pair · long, n 3, unproven. The score is high because the condition is very unusual, not because it has ever paid.

So: A gets sized to its void (117.2, about 3% away) and B gets ignored — or watched, to see whether it earns a record. The score ordered them; n decided.

11Calendar

Value

The two things that reliably blow up a well-reasoned position: an earnings print and a macro release. This is where you see them coming, and how this name has behaved through prints before.

How to use it
  1. Upcoming — the next two weeks of earnings for the universe, grouped by day with the session: BMO before the open, AMC after the close. Starred names float to the top of each day.
  2. Reported — the last two days stay on the tab with actual vs estimate, beat/miss, and the print's own reaction candle. The tape's verdict is the move beside the EPS verdict; they often disagree.
  3. Reaction study — for each name: how many prints, the average and median move the next session, how often up vs down, gap behaviour, and the move as a multiple of the name's usual daily range. That last one is the sizing number.
  4. Macro rows — FOMC, CPI, payrolls, PPI, retail sales, GDP, PCE, with the prior print shown ahead of the release and prior → actual after.
  • You do not have to open the tab to be warned: the E badge on the Markets ticker cell (solid = today, hollow = tomorrow) and the macro banner under the nav read the same calendar.
  • Indices, FX, commodities and baskets never report — the coverage line says how many names are eligible so absence is not a mystery.
In real life — Sunday night planning

You open Calendar. Four of your starred names report this week. PLTR is Tuesday AMC; its reaction study says median 9%, up 5 / down 7, 2.8× usual range. FOMC is Wednesday 2pm.

Your 'short PLTR' idea just became 'short PLTR after the print, or half size into it with the 9% as the risk number'. And nothing new goes on Wednesday afternoon.

So: the week's plan takes five minutes, and the E badges on Markets will keep reminding you as each day arrives.

12News

Value

Answers "why is this moving" without leaving the site — headlines tied to names in the universe, the macro tape and SEC filings on one feed.

How to use it
  1. Filter by ticker with /. The drawer's news panel is the same feed, already filtered to the open name.
  2. Read the badges. Amber = earnings within a week; red = an open signal on that name; FL = an SEC filing, with material forms marked.
  3. The window is 72 hours. Older is history, not news.
  • Type news nvda in the Ask console for the same slice as a table.
  • Material filings can also reach you as an alert class — turn it on in the bell.
In real life — why is this down?

TSLA is −4% at 10:10 ET with nothing obvious on the tape. You press /, type TSLA in the news filter: an FL-badged 8-K landed twenty minutes ago.

So: you have the reason in ten seconds without opening another site — and if you had the filing alert class on, you would have known before the price told you.

13AI Report

Value

The whole board on one name, written out in sentences — with scenarios priced, an action block whose expected value is checked by the server, and a track record of its own past reads.

How to use it
  1. Open it from the drawer's AI report →, the Report tab with a focused ticker, or Shift+Enter in the command palette.
  2. Read the picture and the flags first, then the scenarios. The colour follows the money: an adverse scenario renders red even if the model called it a "target".
  3. Weigh the action block the way you weigh a card: its EV-at-entry is computed server-side, and a plan that prices negative is downgraded to wait before it reaches you.
  4. Check the track record at the bottom — every directional read is ledgered and graded out of sample like any signal.
  5. Regenerate only when something material changed; the button unlocks on a cooldown or on a moved mark, a fresh signal or a print.
  • Reports are shared: the first person to generate one caches it for everyone. Generation is capped per person and per group per day, so do not spend it on a name nothing happened to.
  • Sector and basket reports exist too: report sector Semis, report basket AAPL MSFT NVDA.
  • It is a reading of numbers other tabs already show. If it cites something you cannot find on the board, that is a reason to trust it less, not more.
In real life — five minutes before a meeting

You type report HOOD. The picture: crowded long, funding at its monthly max, price pressing a level with three signals open. Scenarios priced. The action block says wait — the EV of entering here priced negative, so the server downgraded the plan before it reached you.

At the bottom: seven past reads on this name, five right.

So: you do not chase it into the meeting. The report did not tell you what will happen; it told you what the board already said, in sentences, and refused to dress up a bad entry.

14Notes

Value

Your own thinking on a name, stamped with the price it was written at — so next month you read "wrote it at 113.90 · +4.0% since" instead of trying to remember what you meant.

How to use it
  1. Open the drawer on a name and click ＋ note in the Notes panel above the charts.
  2. Write what you actually think — a level, a reason, a rule for yourself. One honest line beats a paragraph of hedging.
  3. Tag it with #tags in the body: #thesis, #level, #mistake. Tags are read out of the text, so you never have to manage them.
  4. Save. The note is stamped with the mark and the time, once.
  5. Find it again on the Notes taboperator-only by default — newest first, searchable over bodies and tickers, filterable by tag — or by the ◢ post-it in the Markets ticker cell (bright within a week, dimmer to a month, hollow after). ◢ noted in Filters narrows the table to names you have written on.
  • Editing changes the words and nothing else. The timestamp and the price stand, and the edit is disclosed. If your view changed, write a new note — that is the record you will want.
  • A name rotated out of the universe keeps its note, greyed and labelled.
  • Writing needs the operator to have turned on note-writing for you; reading the tab never grants the pen.
  • A call in Messages can be written into the notes book with one click, keeping its stamp.
In real life — your own record, kept honest

Aug 30: you wrote on HOOD, at 113.90, "if it loses 108 the thesis is wrong". Sep 14 it is 121 and paying 58% funding; you write "not adding here".

A month later you re-read both. The first is +4.0% since, the second −2.5% since. The second call was the better one, and you can see it without remembering anything.

So: the note book becomes the one place your reasoning is stored at the price it was made — which is the only way to learn whether your reasoning is any good.

15Messages

Value

A conversation where every call carries the price it was made at, forever. The group's shared record — with the board's own tables postable straight into the thread.

How to use it
  1. Pick a conversation in the rail: a one-to-one with a member, a group, or a topic board anyone can join. Groups are created from the rail with a name; topic boards are standing posts for an idea, a ticker thesis, a theme.
  2. Name tickers with a dollar sign. $HOOD is stamped with the price at the moment you send it, and every later read shows the move since. Write short $HOOD and it scores as a short; everything else scores as a long.
  3. Post a table instead of describing one. /top funding 5, /nvda, /screen rvol>2, /earnings today — the result lands in the thread under your name, badged computed. /help shows you the verbs privately.
  4. Share a piece of the board. Hover a cell on Markets for the ⤴ at its corner, hover the ticker cell for the row, right-click for cell · row · this screen, or press s on a highlighted row. The sheet previews the card, picks the conversation, takes a note, and can quote it as a call.
  5. Bind an alert to the conversation. /alert NVDA > 200, /alert NVDA crosses down 180, /alert any rvol > 3 — when it fires, the fire posts here. /alert list, /alert off <id>.
  6. Review the record. calls ↗ in the rail lists every stamped call with the direction-adjusted move since and at fixed 1-day and 7-day horizons, per person. Click a person to filter to their calls. Deleting a call removes the words, never the stamp or the score.
Getting it on your phone
  • Escalation. An unread message reaches your linked Telegram after five minutes — unless you are online, or the thread is muted. An @mention or a message about a ticker on your watch list (the per-person list in the rail, separate from ★) goes immediately and pierces a mute.
  • Telegram sync (⇄) in a conversation's header mirrors that one conversation both ways: every message lands on your phone as it happens, and plain text you send the bot posts here under your name. One conversation at a time.
  • Browser notifications — enable them in the rail and unread messages arrive with the tab closed.
  • Replies quote one line and click back; pins sit in a strip at the top; reactions are a small fixed set; drafts survive a reload; a conversation exports as JSON.
  • Attachments: images and plain-text notes, checked by the server itself, not by what the file claims to be. Paste a screenshot straight into the box.
  • A call can be armed as a price alert at its stamp — fires when the market crosses back through the level it was called at — or written into your notes book with one click.
Read this once. On this deployment the operator can read every conversation, including ones they are not in. Every such read is written to an audit log the operator panel shows. Write accordingly.
In real life — no argument about who said what

2pm: cara posts adding $HOOD here — stamped 113.90. gus answers short $PLTR into the print — stamped 151.30. Someone types /top funding 3 and the live table lands in the thread for everyone.

18Alerts and Telegram

Value

You do not have to watch the screen. Tell the server what matters and it tells you — in the tab, on your phone, or as a morning brief.

Set a rule (two minutes)
  1. Click the 🔔 bell in the status line, open Rules.
  2. Ticker (leave blank for any market) · metric · above / below · threshold. Thresholds are in the metric's own unit: 5 means 5%, 50 means 50M.
  3. Save. It is evaluated on the server against the same snapshot the board renders, so it fires with no tab open. One crossing is one alert (there is a cooldown), and you can hold up to 60.
Rules that earn their keep any · funding percentile · > 90 — a market's crowd is paying near its monthly max.
any · relative volume · > 3 — something is being traded that is not normally traded.
NVDA · price · < 180 — your level, without staring at it.
any · ΔOI 1d · > 15 — money arrived somewhere today.
Link Telegram (once)
  1. Bell → Delivery → link. The panel shows a single-use code (ten minutes).
  2. Send /start CODE to the deployment's bot. It replies Linked.
  3. Pick the alert classes you want on your phone and set quiet hours (a local range like 23-7). test sends a probe.
Choose what reaches you
  • On by default: new setups on the actionable board (with your own EV / reward:risk / lateness thresholds and per-name mutes), ledger outcomes (a void taken, a target reached, a horizon resolved), and your rules.
  • Opt in: trend changes, 200-day crosses, material SEC filings, earnings on names you have a claim on, macro prints, a flipped AI stance, tape-wide crowding extremes, feed outages. Each shows how often it actually fires, so you can judge the noise before you subscribe.
  • Scheduled sends: the morning brief (daily, at your hour), the Landscape commentary a few times a week, and a desk digest of your own calls and how they are scoring. Set the hour and the days per send.
  • Recent in the bell is the server's own record — it survives a refresh, a closed tab and a redeploy. Clearing it only hides it in this browser.
  • Delivery is paced (a message every few seconds, a cap per hour): held, not dropped, and disclosed in the panel.
  • /stop to the bot unlinks, always — control of the Telegram account outranks any session.
In real life — acting on the rule, not the feeling

You write two rules: HOOD · price · < 108 (the level your own note said would void the thesis) and any · funding percentile · > 90. You link Telegram, quiet hours 23-7.

Wednesday 3:12pm, phone buzzes: HOOD 107.80 < 108. You are not watching the screen. You do what you said you would do three weeks ago, at the price you said.

So: the rule turned a plan into an action, and the funding-percentile rule keeps handing you the crowded-trade names without you hunting for them.

20Ten habits that make it pay

  1. Check the window before you react to a number. Most "that can't be right" moments are a window you forgot you set.
  2. Hover before you conclude. The sample size and the inputs are one hover away on every cell.
  3. Sort by vs tape, not by 24h. Relative strength on a red day is the read that matters.
  4. Read n before score on every signal card.
  5. Search a name's claim history before you trust a card on it.
  6. Look at the funding heatmap before you hold anything overnight. Carry is a cost you can see coming.
  7. Check correlation before you add a name. The third chip stock is not a third idea.
  8. Write the note now, at the price. Future you will read it with the move since.
  9. Let rules watch levels so you do not have to.
  10. Trust the record, read the card. The out-of-sample ledger is the part of this site that cannot flatter you.
