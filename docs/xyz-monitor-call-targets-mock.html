Call targets: "INTC to 32 by Oct 15", tracked to the day it resolves

A call today is a direction and a horizon: it closes when the clock runs out. A target is a stronger claim — a price and a date — and it resolves on whichever comes first: the mark reaches the level (hit), the date passes (miss), or an optional invalidation level prints first (wrong). The stamp grows a progress row that answers the only question anyone asks about a target: how far along is it, and is it ahead of its clock or behind it.

◆ mock · synthetic prices, nothing is wired to live data

1 · Writing one  the composer — the grammar is a sentence, the preview shows what it understood

Edit the message · the preview chip re-parses as you type · try $INTC to 32 by Oct 15 · $INTC 32 in 3w · short $TSLA to 300 by 10/31 unless 360 · $BTC 120k by year end

What the grammar takes. A ticker, then to / → / nothing, a price (32, $32.50, 120k), then a deadline: by a date (Oct 15, 10/15, 2026-10-15, Friday, year end) or in a span (3w, 10d, 2 months). An optional unless / wrong under / stop level. Direction is inferred from the target (above the mark = long, below = short) and the existing short words still win. Anything the grammar cannot read stays a plain call with the 7-day horizon — never a wrong target.

2 · In the conversation  the stamp grows a target row — drag the clock to watch four targets resolve
days since sentday 6

Reading the row. The bar is price progress from the sent mark to the target (red and leftward when the mark went the wrong way); the small triangle under it is time used. Bar ahead of the triangle: ahead of its clock. A red tick on the bar is the invalidation level. The right-hand number stays the raw move since sent, exactly as a plain call; the status pill says what the target did: open, hit (with how early), missed (with how close it got), wrong (the stop printed first), or closed early by the author.

Two records, honestly separated. The % record stays what it is today (was the direction right at the close, and by how much). Targets add a second, binary record — hit / miss / wrong — that a plain call cannot have, because it never said where it was going. A missed target that got 71% of the way is still a miss on the binary record and still +11.5% on the move record; both are true, and the board says both. Median time-to-hit is the number nobody has today and everybody would use.

5 · What this pins down
// three columns on the stamped row, beside callH / closedAt / closePx from the lifecycle
tgPx    REAL      // the target level; direction follows from its side of refPx unless the words say otherwise
tgBy    INTEGER   // the deadline: end of that day, in the member's timezone, stored as UTC ms
tgStop  REAL      // optional invalidation level; NULL = none
tgRes   TEXT      // 'hit' | 'miss' | 'wrong' | 'early', written ONCE by the resolver; NULL while open
tgAt    INTEGER   // when it resolved; closePx is the price it resolved at (target, stop, or the deadline's close)

// resolution, checked on the 5-minute bars the level scanner already reads (and the daily close for the deadline):
//   hit   — a bar's high (long) / low (short) reaches tgPx before tgBy and before tgStop ever prints
//   wrong — a bar reaches tgStop first
//   miss  — the daily close of tgBy prints with neither touched; closePx = that close
//   early — the author closed it (the lifecycle's close), closePx = the live mark
// progress (client, live): (mark − refPx) / (tgPx − refPx), clamped to [−1, 1]; time used: (now − ts) / (tgBy − ts)
  • A target is a call with more said. Same row, same stamp, same record; the lifecycle's horizon becomes the deadline, and the horizon close becomes the deadline close. Nothing about plain calls changes.
  • Hits are intraday, misses are at the close. A target is a level, and levels are touched, not closed through: the 5-minute bars the level alerts already scan decide a hit or a stop. The deadline is a date, and dates end at the close. Both rules are the ones a trader would state unprompted.
  • The resolver is the level scanner. A target registers the same way a stamp's ⚑ alert does today (a bound rule at the level), so a hit fires through the alert engine into the conversation with no second scanner. The deadline check rides the daily-close tick that prints the 7d horizons.
  • The record grows a second column, it does not replace one. hit / miss / wrong per person, median time-to-hit, and the plain % record beside them. A missed target is a miss and a +11.5% at the same time.
  • Extend and close early still work. Extending a target moves its deadline; closing it early resolves it as "early" at the mark, which is neither a hit nor a miss — the binary record leaves it out.
6 · What is missing before this ships
  • Touch or close for a hit. The mock says intraday touch on 5-minute bars. A wick through the level for one bar is a hit under that rule; some desks would want a close through it. One switch per deployment, or a word in the grammar (close above 32).
  • Time zones for "by Oct 15". The deadline is the end of that day in the author's zone (the alerts panel already knows it). A member with no zone set gets ET, and the stamp says which.
  • Crypto never closes. The deadline close for a coin is the 00:00 UTC daily close the spine already keeps; hits work the same.
  • Editing. A target's numbers are part of the stamp and stay immutable under an edit, exactly as the price stamp does today. Change the target by closing early and writing a new one.
  • Partial credit. Deliberately none on the binary record. The % record already gives it.
