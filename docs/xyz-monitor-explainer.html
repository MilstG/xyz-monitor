What this thing does, in plain words

A tour of the terminal for the people who use it: what each screen is for, what the numbers actually say, what is yours to keep, and what the app deliberately will not do. No setup, no configuration, no code — that is the complete manual. Start here, go there when you want the exact definition of a column.

Nothing here is investment advice. Everything below is a description of what the software computes and shows.

01What it is

Milst Screener watches the perpetual futures markets on Hyperliquid's xyz dex — synthetic perps that track US and foreign stocks, indices, FX, commodities and baskets — plus a second, separate universe of the main-dex crypto perps. It is a read-only monitor: it shows you what the tape is doing, keeps a record of the conditions it flagged, and tells you when something you asked about happens. It cannot place an order, and it never holds a key that could.

The design has one central idea, and knowing it explains most of what you will see: the heavy work happens once, on the server. A small process polls Hyperliquid, archives candles, samples open interest, computes every feature and caches the result. Your browser reads a single finished snapshot. Nobody's browser talks to the exchange, so the page opens in seconds instead of minutes, and everyone is looking at the same numbers at the same time.

The snapshot

The market table is rebuilt about every 15 seconds and served whole. The updated field in the status line is the server's last successful poll — not your last refresh.

The live stream

While the status line reads push live, the server tells your browser the moment something changed and the browser fetches it. The refresh selector is the fallback for when that stream drops.

The archive

Open interest, funding history, candles, the signal ledger, your notes and messages live on a disk that survives restarts. A redeploy serves a warm table within a poll or two.

02Your first ten minutes

  1. Sign in. You get in through a single-use invite link from the operator, pick a handle and a password, and that account is yours across devices. Forgot it? The reset code goes to your linked Telegram.
  2. Land on Markets and pick a scope. The Stocks | Crypto pills at the top switch between the two universes. They never mix. Whichever you pick is remembered for next time.
  3. Set the analysis window. The 1h · 4h · 1d · 7d · 30d selector re-anchors every window-relative column at once — vs S&P, vs tape, ΔOI, Squeeze, Carry, Avg Range, RVOL all answer "over this window". Most confusion about a number is a window you forgot you set.
  4. Hover anything that puzzles you. Every number in the app explains itself on hover: exact inputs, the window it was measured over, the sample size behind it. Press ? for the "how to read this tab" card, which links into the manual.
  5. Open a name. Click a row (or press Enter on a highlighted one) and the ticker drawer slides in: candles, positioning, event risk, co-movers, news, the signal record, your notes. That name becomes the focused ticker — every other tab now opens on it.
  6. Star a few names. ★ pins a market to your watchlist, floats it to the top of the table, and follows your account onto your phone.
  7. Arrange the table once, then save it. The ⚙ column menu shows, hides and reorders columns; Layouts saves that arrangement (columns, sort, window, filters, ★-only) under a name you choose. Phones start from a mobile preset and can keep their own layout.
  8. Ask a question instead of hunting. Press ~ (or the ▸_ Ask button) and type top funding 5, nvda, most crowded shorts, whats above the 200dma.
  9. Set one alert. The 🔔 bell in the status line holds your rules: ticker, metric, above/below, threshold. It is evaluated on the server against the same snapshot the board renders, so it fires with no tab open. Link Telegram there and it reaches your phone.
  10. Write one note. In the drawer, above the charts. It is stamped with the price it was written at, so every later read says "wrote it at 113.90 · +4.0% since". That one field is the whole point.

03Six ideas that explain every screen

1 · Two universes, one wall

Stocks and crypto are separate worlds with separate benchmarks (the S&P 500 perp; BTC), separate histories and separate visibility. Session concepts — gaps, overnight holds, cash sessions — do not exist on a 24/7 tape and simply vanish in crypto scope.

why a column disappeared

2 · The window re-anchors everything

One control, many columns. A "squeeze of 70" is a 70 over the window you chose. The action strip, the sector cells and the CSV export all describe exactly the rows and window on screen.

why two people see different numbers

3 · One number, one code path

A level, a score or a record is computed once on the server and restated everywhere. The board, the drawer, the alert and the Telegram message cannot disagree, because none of them recomputes. Your browser only re-times the live mark.

why the alert matches the card

4 · A dash is a dash

Missing history, an unqualified baseline, a retired data series — all render as an explicit blank. Never a guess, never an interpolation, never a zero standing in for "we don't know".

why crypto shows dashes past MA20

5 · A signal is a condition, not a prediction

Every flagged condition is ranked by how unusual it is now plus what happened the last times it occurred — with the sample size, the median outcome and the hit rate stated on the card. Each one is ledgered at the mark it fired at and scored out of sample afterwards. The card is the part to read; the record is the part to trust.

how to weigh anything the engine says

6 · Prose carries its price

A note or a message that names a ticker is stamped with the mark at the moment it was written. Editing rewrites the words and nothing else — the timestamp and the stamp stand, and the edit is disclosed. A call cannot quietly become a better call.

why notes beat a text file

04The screens, and when to open each

Every tab is one question asked of the same rows. Tabs marked operator-only by default ship closed to the group; if you cannot see one, it is not hidden from you by accident — the operator has not opened it, and its data routes refuse too.

The tape

Markets

The home board: one row per market, ~40 columns, sortable, filterable, exportable. Everything else in the app is a different cut of these rows.

start here, always

Ticker drawer

One name, every surface at once: candles, where the 30d return happened, positioning, event risk, co-movers and hedges, fundamentals, news, your notes, the signal record.

"tell me about this name"

Trend

An EMA 13/21 ladder on four timeframes per name, scored 0–4 by how many agree, ranked into long and short boards. RETEST marks the pullback into the ribbon.

"what is actually trending, and where is the entry"

Charts

Up to eight names side by side, or one name across timeframes in a multi-timeframe wall, with linked crosshair and zoom.

comparing shapes, not numbers

Treemap

The universe as tiles: area is volume or open interest, colour is the move over your window.

the ten-second read of the day

Sectors

Where capital is flowing: a rotation flow map, leaders, cohesion (does the label trade as one block?) and a sector × sector correlation matrix.

"which theme is being bought"

Correlation

What moves with what: a cluster-ordered matrix, pair detail with hedge β and a spread z-score, co-movers and natural hedges, rebased overlays and synthetic ratio candles.

before you double a bet you already have

Funding

Carry across the whole book over calendar time. Red = longs pay, green = longs receive; a bucket with too little data is hatched, never drawn as flat. Each row ends with its window mean beside its current funding.

"is the crowd paying more or less than it was"

Sessions

When the returns actually happen: overnight vs cash vs weekend holds as equity curves, net of the funding drag, plus hour-of-day and day-of-week clocks.

"when should I be holding this"

Signals and setups

Signals

Unusual conditions, ranked and audited. Every card states the evidence in one fixed grammar — sample size, median outcome, hit rate, expectancy, horizon — plus the mechanical side, void and target. Search any ticker for its whole claim-by-claim history.

"what is unusual right now, and has it ever mattered"

Actionableoperator

Only the names sitting at a trigger right now, with the entry, invalidation and target you would actually use — and reward:risk stated net of expected funding.

the morning list

Focusoperator

Six equities, stamped once at the cash open and then frozen for the day. A compressed morning scan that deliberately never reshuffles because a number ticked.

first fifteen minutes of the US day

Backtestoperator

Cross-sectional long/short tests on the daily returns already in your browser, with an out-of-sample split, costs and funding — plus the running duel between the two momentum scores.

"does this ranking rule hold up"

Context and calendar

Calendar

Earnings for the next two weeks with the session (before the open, after the close), prints from the last two days with beat/miss and the reaction move, each name's own earnings reaction study, and the macro prints — FOMC, CPI, payrolls, PPI, retail sales, GDP, PCE.

before you size anything into a print

News

72 hours of headlines tied to names in the universe, merged with the macro tape and SEC filings, badged where a name has earnings or an open signal.

"why is this moving"

Housing · Liquidityoperator

The macro backdrop: mortgage rates, starts, supply and non-QM spreads; and Fed net liquidity with the TGA and reverse-repo drains.

the weather behind the tape

Congress · Insidersoperator

STOCK Act disclosures (amounts are the disclosed band, never a point figure) and SEC Form 4 transactions (exact shares, exact price, exact date), both sorted by when the market learned.

who was buying their own name

Your own work

AI Report

Everything the server holds on one name compiled into a written read: the picture, what the data says, scenarios with computed reward:risk, a mandatory action block, what would change the read. Every number it cites is a number another tab shows, and every directional read is ledgered and graded like any other claim.

when you want it in sentences

Notesoperator

Every note you have written, newest first, searchable and filterable by the #tags in the bodies. Each one carries the mark it was written at and the move since. A name rotated out of the universe keeps its note.

"what did I think last time"

Messages

Conversations between account holders where a $TICKER is stamped with the price at the moment you sent it — so a call reads "sent at 113.90 · +4.0%" forever. calls ↗ collects every stamped message with its scored move, per person. Slash commands post live tables into the thread; you can mirror a conversation to Telegram both ways.

the group's shared record

Fundsoperator

Tracked 13F filers with their books and quarter-over-quarter deltas. Positioning history filed up to 45 days late — never the current book, and labelled as such.

slow money, read slowly

05Reading the numbers you will meet most

Exact formulas live in the manual and on every hover. These are the plain-English readings.

What you seeWhat it is telling you
Funding (APR)Positive/red = longs pay to hold the position — the crowd is long and carry is a cost. Negative/green = longs get paid. A ▴/▾ flag means today's rate sits at the top or bottom decile of this market's own last month.
PremHow far the perp has dislocated from its oracle price, in basis points. When the underlying cash market is closed, a persistent premium is the off-hours price discovery.
ΔOI + its tagWhether money is arriving or leaving, read against price: longs+ new money confirming · squeeze shorts covering, not fresh demand · shorts+ new money pressing lower · unwind longs deleveraging. Open interest is symmetric, so which side owns it is always an inference — and the hover says so.
RVOLVolume against the same clock hours over the past month. 1.0× is normal for this time of day; 2×+ is genuinely elevated. The session shape cannot masquerade as a signal.
Momentum · MOM+A self-normalizing −100…+100 score, comparable across a sleepy FX pair and a violent synthetic. MOM+ is the challenger being scored head-to-head against it every day, in public, on the Backtest tab.
SqueezeHow loaded the short-squeeze spring is: shorts paying, open interest building, price pressing the 30-day high. Zero whenever funding is positive — no crowded shorts, no squeeze.
CarryFunding per unit of volatility: how much you are paid for taking the unpopular side, adjusted for how wild the ride is.
vs S&P · vs tapeExcess return over the benchmark, and over the universe median. On a day when everything is red, "vs tape" is the honest relative-strength read.
OI / VolPositioning divided by turnover. ≥2 means crowded positioning that cannot exit quickly; under 0.5 is fresh churn.
DownCap · Hit%How much of the tape's drop this name eats on red bars, and how consistently. A flattering DownCap with a low Hit% means a couple of lucky bars carried it.
vs VWAPWhere price sits against the month's volume-weighted average — roughly, whether recent buyers are in profit or trapped.
BetaHow much of the benchmark's move this name typically takes: above 1 amplifies, below 1 dampens, negative moves inverse.

Badges in the ticker cell

MarkMeaning
★ / ☆Watchlist. Follows your account across devices.
EEarnings: solid = reports today, hollow = tomorrow. After the print it becomes a scoreboard — beat/miss and the day's move.
◢You have a note on this name. Bright within a week, dimmer to a month, a hollow outline after; a count past one. Hover for the first line and the move since.
⬡Your linked wallet holds a position here, in the side's colour.

07Asking instead of hunting

Press ~ or click ▸_ Ask. The console runs over the rows already in your browser, so answers are instant. Tab completes verbs, fields and tickers; ↑ ↓ walk your history; every result names the rows it ran on.

Type thisGet
nvdaThe name's card — price, day move, funding, OI, squeeze, momentum, vs tape.
nvda fundingOne column on one name.
top funding 5Any column ranked, plus gainers, losers, trending.
screen rvol>2 & squeeze>50A filter across the board, fields joined with &.
breadth · sectorsHow healthy the tape is; which sectors are working.
earnings today · news nvdaThe calendar and the 72-hour headline window.
vs nvda amd · corr nvda amdSide-by-side compare; correlation and hedge β.

report nvdaOpen or generate the written analyst read.
most crowded shorts · hows the tapePlain English — common questions are translated into the grammar and the translation is echoed back so you can see what it ran.
Anything the local grammar cannot parse goes to the AI leg, where the operator has enabled it. That leg is metered: the ask chip shows the group's remaining calls for the day, and a 🔒 means generation is locked until an operator unlocks it. Local verbs never touch the budget.

The same verbs, in a conversation

In any Messages thread, type / and the verb — /top funding 5, /nvda, /screen rvol>2, /earnings today — and the result posts into the thread under your name, badged computed, so the group reads one table instead of six people opening the panel. Two extras only exist here:

  • /alert NVDA > 200, /alert NVDA crosses down 180, /alert any rvol > 3 — a threshold rule bound to that conversation: when it fires, the fire posts there. /alert list and /alert off <id> manage them.
  • /share <ticker>, or the ⤴ that appears when you hover a cell — posts any cell, row or screen of the markets table as a data card, optionally quoted as a call.

08Being told, instead of watching

The 🔔 bell lives in the status line, so it is reachable from every tab. One event stream feeds every channel — the in-tab toast, the bell panel, Telegram, browser push — so a channel's settings can quiet your phone without ever editing the record of what fired.

Your own rules

Ticker (or blank for any market) · metric · above or below · threshold. Price, the window returns, funding and its percentile, premium, volume, open interest, relative volume, ΔOI, liquidations. Evaluated on the server against the same snapshot the board renders, with an arm band and a cooldown so one crossing is one alert.

Classes you opt into

Setups and ledger outcomes are on by default. Trend changes, 200-day crosses, material SEC filings, earnings on names you have a claim open on, macro prints, a flipped AI stance, tape-wide crowding extremes and feed outages are each a switch, shown with how often they actually fire.

Telegram

Link once from the bell (a single-use code, ten minutes), then pick classes and quiet hours. Unread messages escalate to your phone after five minutes — unless you are online, or the thread is muted, or someone @mentioned you, which pierces the mute.

Scheduled reading

A morning brief (indices, movers, sectors, positioning, earnings, macro, headlines), a written market commentary a few times a week, and a deterministic per-person desk digest of your own calls and how they are scoring. You set the hour and the days.

10What it will not do

Knowing the edges saves more time than any feature.

  • It cannot trade. A linked wallet is a public address. The app reads public state and holds nothing that could sign an order.
  • It does not predict. A signal is an unusual condition with a measured history attached, ranked. Where there is no history the card says unproven; where the history lost money it says neg exp and ranks it as noise.
  • It does not fill gaps. Crypto history is kept for 31 days by design, so long moving averages and year-to-date columns there honestly show dashes.
  • Some data is old on purpose. 13F books are quarter-end positions filed up to 45 days late; congressional disclosures land up to 45 days after the trade; ETF composition trails by a month or two. Each is labelled where it is shown.
  • Some data is context, not truth. Aggregated liquidations and cross-exchange open interest describe the wider market, not the Hyperliquid book, and say so on the panel.
  • Open-interest attribution is an inference. OI is symmetric; "longs building" is a reading of price against OI, and the hover always tells you what it rests on.
  • The AI is a reader, not an oracle. It is a synthesis of numbers other tabs already show, it is capped per person and per group per day, and its directional reads are graded out of sample like every other claim.
  • Messages are not private from the operator on this deployment. Every operator read is written to an audit log that is shown in the admin panel. Write accordingly.
  • It is not advice. Every screen describes what the software measured.

11When something looks wrong

The dot beside the wordmark is amber.
The server's data has gone stale — its poller stalled. Your connection is fine; the numbers are just older than they should be. Red means your browser cannot reach the server at all.
A column is full of dashes.
There is not enough history to answer honestly — a new listing, a crypto name past the 31-day retention, or a window longer than the data. The app will not interpolate to look complete.
A number here disagrees with a number there.
It usually isn't the same question: check the analysis window, the scope pill, and whether you are in the group lens. Everything else is computed once on the server and restated, so genuine disagreement would be a bug worth reporting.
A tab I was told about isn't in the ribbon.
The operator has not opened that feature to the group. Ask them — it is one switch, no deploy.
My phone shows different columns than my desktop.
By design. Phones start from the mobile preset, and which layout is active is per browser. Your saved layouts and watchlist do sync.
The build stamp looks old.
The status line's build is the server's. If it does not match the latest delivery, the deploy is stale — that is an operator question, not a browser one.
My note "moved" after I edited it.
It did not. The timestamp and the price stamp are written once; only the words changed, and the edit is disclosed.
