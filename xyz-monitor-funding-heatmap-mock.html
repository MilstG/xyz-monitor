The cross-section of carry, over calendar time

The positioning stack could already say what carry is doing now (the regime aggregate) and when in the day it concentrates (the funding clock). Neither showed the whole book's carry building or decaying across days — which is where a crowded side actually announces itself: one row going hot for two days while everything else sits flat. This is that view: a row per market, a column per time bucket, readable at 1h, 8h or 24h. The decision the rest of this page defends is that the timeframe changes the quantity, not the zoom.

2 · The timeframe is a quantity  the one idea the whole study rests on

One market, one moment, read three ways. The buttons are not a zoom control.

◆ SMCI — the same carry, three horizons
◆ one day, three units — the same payments, repiled

A cell is the funding a 1× long actually paid over that bucket — the hourly spine summed across it, not an hourly rate re-labelled. Hyperliquid pays hourly, so an 8h bucket is eight payments and a 24h bucket is twenty-four; the same market genuinely reads ~8× and ~24× larger. That is why each timeframe carries its own colour cap, shipped from the server as the 98th percentile of that grid's own |cells|. Reuse one cap across the buttons and the 1h grid goes to near-black while the 24h grid saturates solid — the numbers would be right and the picture would be useless. Bucket width also sets the horizon, so the button picks a question as well as a unit: 1h → 2 days (did carry spike overnight), 8h → 14 days (the funding-epoch view a desk actually quotes), 24h → 30 days (is this a regime or a week).

3 · A gap is not a zero  the honesty problem this study had to solve first

Same market, same window, same spine. Only the treatment of the missing hours differs.

◆ shipped — unknown is hatched

◆ rejected — missing hours summed short

The spine is routinely partial. It is seeded from ~5-minute OI samples deduped to the hour, topped up by a best-effort fundingHistory backfill whose support on a HIP-3 dex is not guaranteed, and quiet markets simply skip hours. Sum a bucket that holds three of its eight hours and the cell reads carry collapsed when the truth is we saw three hours — the right-hand panel, where a coverage hole is indistinguishable from a market that went flat, and the two mean opposite things to anyone sizing a position. So a partial bucket is scaled to full width (mean observed rate × N hours), and a bucket under half coverage returns null and is drawn as a hatch. The same rule protects the right edge: the newest column is the last complete bucket, because a half-elapsed one would paint a fake collapse in carry down every row in the book, every render.

4 · The sign is never colour-only  red/green is the hard pair, and we kept it anyway

Toggle the deuteranopia simulation — the hues collapse toward each other, and the grid still reads.

vision approximate deuteranope simulation, applied as an SVG filter

Colour carries the direction, but never alone. Red = longs pay, green = longs receive, neutral midpoint = the panel itself — the app's standing carry semantic, shared with the funding clock two panels down and the correlation matrix a tab away; inventing a fourth palette for the same concept would cost more than the CVD margin it bought. Run that pair through a palette validator and the adjacent-hue separation lands at ΔE ≈ 6.7 under deuteranopia — the floor band, legal only with a second encoding. So the direction is carried twice more without colour: every row is direct-labelled with its signed mean (one shared decimal count per grid, derived from that timeframe's cap, so the column aligns and a 1h grid is not forced into a 24h grid's decimals), and every cell's tooltip names the side in words. A value that rounds away at the grid's precision prints ≈0% rather than a signed string of zeros — the sign is the encoding, and it must not claim a direction the number does not have.

5 · What this pins down

Buckets belong to the epoch, not to the request

Every bucket start is a multiple of its own width — floor(end / width) * width — so two markets built a second apart land on the same columns and the grid has one shared time axis instead of sixty slightly-offset ones.

The alternative (anchor to "now", walk back N buckets) is the version that looks identical in a screenshot and silently makes every row incomparable to every other row.

The cap is shipped, not derived client-side

A percentile computed over the visible rows would repaint every survivor whenever you sorted or trimmed the list — the same cell changing colour because you changed the sort is a chart lying about its own data.

So the server computes one cap per timeframe over the whole grid and ships it. Sorting, re-ranking and the row-count control move rows around; they never touch a colour.

The grid moves the ETag

The columns re-cut every time a bucket closes, even when the roster, the coverage and every rate are unchanged. So the newest 1h column start joins the board's own content signature.

Without it, a book that stopped listing new markets would serve a cached payload and the right edge of the heatmap would quietly freeze — the one failure mode nobody reports, because a stale chart looks exactly like a quiet market.

Ranked by open interest, capped at 60

Rows past the cap are a scrollbar, not a signal, and the crypto book is large enough to make that a real cost in payload and in reading time. OI is the ranking because carry on a market nobody holds is trivia.

The client shows 25 by default and says top 60 of N by OI only when the cap actually bound the list — a market dropped for a spine too thin to plot is not a market the cap hid.

A tab of its own means a route of its own

It first shipped as one section of the Sessions payload. As a board in its own right that was untenable: opening the tab pulled the entire session study set — levels, anatomy, seasonality, the decomposition — to paint one grid, and waited on the analytics build cadence to do it.

/api/funding is lazy and memoized on the same contract /api/trend uses, one board per universe, with a scope-prefixed validator so a crypto request can never 304 against a cached stocks body.

It reuses the funding spine already on disk

No new fetch, no new persistence, no new rate-limit weight. The hourly funding history was already banked for the boundary engine's net-of-funding math; this study is a second reading of it.

Which is also why it works on day one of a redeploy: the spine is warm-loaded from the volume, so the grid arrives populated rather than accruing for a week.

6 · What it deliberately does not do

It does not rank crowding

A hot row is a hot row; the study will not tell you it is the most crowded side in the book. That claim belongs to the regime aggregate, which already measures it against each market's own 31d percentile — a raw rate compared across markets is a comparison of tick sizes as much as of positioning.

It does not net carry against the move

The obvious next panel overlays the return, so you can see a name that paid you 0.04%/8h to hold something that fell 6%. Deliberately out of scope: that is a hold study, the boundary engine already owns holds, and grafting a return axis onto a positioning grid would make it a worse version of both.

Still open

Whether the row cap should follow the viewport rather than sitting at 60 — on a wide screen the grid can honestly carry more, and the number is a starting proposal.

Whether a row should be clickable through to that ticker's drawer. It obviously should; it is one wiring change and it is not in this build.
